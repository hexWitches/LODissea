Portugal's data is biased

Portugal's GLAM participation rate (7.22%) is inflated because Wikidata only records 554 physical institutions — far below the 3,395 counted in the official INE national census.

When we correct the denominator using the census baseline, the rate drops to ≈ 1.18% — repositioning Portugal's bubble significantly to the left and revealing a very different picture.

The Crew

Who sends knowledge to the Europeana sea?

Behind the vast archive of Europeana stand in total 1002 providers from the selected six countries each loading their cultural treasures onto the digital vessel. Yet the fleet is far from evenly loaded: in several countries, a single flagship carries the volume of the cargo alone. Explore the top-10 providers by country and discover which institutions navigate Europeana's waters most heavily laden.

Boats are not all built the same. A library and a natural history museum may carry equally vast cargo, but what fills their holds - books and manuscripts, or bones and specimens - could not be more different.

The
Cargo Hold

What kinds of knowledge sail these waters?

Not every vessel sails with the same cargo. Across Europeana's six contributing countries, libraries and archives dominate the hold — accounting for over 60% of all items globally. But beneath this surface, each national fleet has its own character: the Netherlands is steered by natural history collections (51%), Portugal mirrors it with 47% scientific heritage, while Italy's hold brims with audiovisual and film archives (31.6%) — a uniquely cinematic identity.
